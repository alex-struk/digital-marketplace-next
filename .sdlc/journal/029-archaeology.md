**Journal**

The content domain of the old marketplace holds the service's own prose — legal notices, program terms and conditions, opportunity scope text, evaluation instructions — as editable pages with a title, a body of formatted text and a short address, managed only by an administrator and readable by anyone. Some pages are ordinary; others are created by migration as pages the service itself depends on, and those may be re-worded but never renamed or removed. Other screens either link to a page by its address or embed its body inline beside their own material, and this run was about what happens on those screens when the page they want cannot be read.

The returned criterion, D-content-26, claimed that a screen existing to show a page's body turns into the not-found screen when its page is missing, blocking the organisation owner from accepting a program's qualification terms. That is inverted. Both program terms screens do carry a guard that reads exactly that way, but it cannot fire: the answer to a page request is a wrapper object that is present whether it carries the page or the reason the page could not be had — 400, 401 and 404 all map to the same wrapper, and a transport failure maps to another — so the emptiness test in that guard is never true and only a missing organisation reaches the not-found screen from there. A terms page that is absent, refused or malformed falls straight past the guard into the ordinary branch, where the body is only assigned on a valid answer, so the screen keeps the empty string it was initialised with and renders the terms title, a blank body and a live Accept Terms & Conditions button. I confirmed the two terms screens are line-for-line identical in this respect, and confirmed the wrapper's shape at its two constructors rather than inferring it from type names.

D-content-26 is now rewritten at v2 to state what those screens actually do, cited to the initialisation, the dead guard, the valid-only assignment, the three view lines that render title, empty body and live button on both programs, the response mapping that makes the guard dead, and the two constructors that make the wrapper always present. Its notes re-site the not-found handling where it genuinely lives — the screen whose whole purpose is showing one page by its address, whose failure branch really does replace the route with not-found, already recorded as R-7.2 — and record that this is the only such screen. The claim that the service picks one handling or the other screen by screen was wrong and is gone; the handling is uniform.

The consequence the old wording buried is now its own criterion, D-content-28, classed as a defect: an organisation's agreement to a program's qualification terms is accepted and recorded even when nothing was shown in place of those terms. The back end's only precondition is that this organisation has not already agreed; it never checks that the terms exist, could be read, or were put in front of the person agreeing, and it then stamps the acceptance date that makes the organisation qualified for that program. There is no corrected criterion to replace it with, because whether a rebuild refuses the acceptance, withholds the action or turns the screen away is a judgement for a human, and it interacts with the separate ability to announce changed terms and require vendors to accept again.

D-content-25 keeps its statement, citations and scenario — I re-read the opportunity screen and the evaluation-instructions tab and both do substitute the empty string on an unreadable page — and gains a note recording that the terms screens behave the same way, replacing the false contrast.

I also revised D-content-24, which the ruling said was now answerable from source. It had been left `open` behind a note asking for a live experiment; that experiment is unnecessary. The submission carries only address, title and body, so no base version travels with it; the new version number is derived from state the service re-reads for itself between validating and writing, and that read is not held together with the write. What the previous wording missed is that the outcome depends on timing: a publish that arrives after another has finished takes the next number and silently wins, but two publishes inside the same window compute the same number and collide on the composite key under which a page's versions are stored, so the second is refused outright and its author is told the change could not be published. The criterion is now v2, `inferred`, with both scenarios and the collision evidence cited.

Two things I could not settle. First, the two terms pages are fixed pages created by migration, so on an installation whose migrations have all run they exist; the behaviour recovered here is reachable when the page has been made unreadable by other means — a failed or refused request, or an installation whose migrations predate the page — and I could not determine from the source how often that occurs in practice, only that the code has no defence against it. Second, whether anything downstream distinguishes an acceptance made against blank terms from one made against real terms: I found no field, flag or log that records which terms were shown, which is itself the finding, but I cannot rule out a record kept outside this domain.

I left the corroborations the ruling volunteered for other criteria alone — the unique-address constraint underneath D-content-11, the enforcement behind D-content-15's note, the absence of any version-history screen behind D-content-13. None of them is contradicted by the evidence, and this run was scoped to the criteria whose evidence was wrong. I did not touch the contract surface or personas files, or any other domain.
